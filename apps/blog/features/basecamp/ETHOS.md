# The Basecamp ethos

Read this first. Before the code, before the glossary, before anything else.

Hive has lost new users for years. A lot of effort went into marketing while the
front door stayed broken. The first part of that door — signing up — is largely
fixed now. The second part is not: people arrive and it is simply not clear what
to do, how to engage, where to go, or how to behave. They post a few times, no
one answers, and they leave. That silence is the void, and it is what Basecamp
exists to end.

At the same time there are long-standing Hive users who would gladly help, and
don't, because finding a real person among the scammers, spammers, bots and
extractors is exhausting. So the
two halves of the problem hold each other still. Basecamp is an attempt to move
both at once.

These are the commitments the project is built on. They are not features and
they are not up for quiet erosion.

## Playfulness, fun and connection

Injecting playfulness, fun and connection into the first year on Hive is the
point. Basecamp should be enjoyable — for new users and for OG users alike.
Gamification is at its core. Anything that is merely structurally useful but
joyless has not finished being designed.

## Open source, and meant to be taken

The project is designed to be worked with by other people. Fork it, run it on
your own front end, take the parts you want, throw the rest away. Iteration by
others is the goal, not a tolerated side effect.

## Collaboration on every level

Not a product with users. A thing people build together. Right now it is one
person and a few friends to argue with; that is the part most in need of
changing.

## Transparency

Information is shown, not withheld.

## Designed for constant iteration and improvement

Light in structure, simple in mechanism, easy to modify. Pieces must not break
other pieces, because the way this gets built is by trying something, looking at
it, deciding it is wrong, and pulling it back out without damage. Dense code that
is slow to load is a failure even when it works.

Nothing here is finished. There may never be a final form — continual iteration,
always seeking dynamic, playful, beautiful and information improvements. Ideally
the adjustments get smaller rather than stop.

## Visual indicators, not conclusions

We provide visual indicators. We do not tell people what to do with those
indicators. Basecamp presents information about a new user; the reader decides
what it means. Earning on Hive and taking your earnings out is not wrongdoing —
people are paid for their work. But the behaviour is visible, everyone can see
it, and the person can speak for themselves. Curators decide. The tool does not
decide for them.
