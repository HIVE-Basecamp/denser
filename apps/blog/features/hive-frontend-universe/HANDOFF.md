# PART B, HUMAN CONTEXT (preserved, not code-derived)

WHO BRYAN IS
Bryan, @buttcoins on Hive. Not a developer. Explain in plain English, no
unexplained jargon. Sharp about product, Hive culture, community politics;
his judgment on those beats the AI's. Percy, his son, originated the

COMMUNICATION
No em dashes ever. Short, direct, dummy-proof steps; over-explain UI
navigation, not concepts. He is usually on iPad; the laptop runs Claude
Code. Nothing pitchy, corporate, or AI-sounding. When he asks for words,
give words not a file. Web addresses go in the browser address bar with the
clicks spelled out. Read pasted output in full. Never claim something works
without verifying and saying what was observed. Never state what is on his
screen, in his repo, or in his settings as fact without seeing it; ask or
say you are guessing. When he asks for the case against something, give it
properly, do not default to agreeing.

THE PROJECT, IN HIS FRAMING
Hive Basecamp: a newcomer section inside hive.blog. People arrive, get no
engagement, leave; veterans retreat from fake extractor accounts; nobody
owns the first month. Basecamp surfaces new accounts, a checklist, guide
matching, gamified bot-spotting. The flagship inside it is the Hive Frontend
Universe: a Zelda-style exploration game that renders 30-minute snapshots of
the live chain as a navigable world. Ethos: easy, fun, collaborative,
modular, no piece breaks another, colorful, simple leads to elaborate,
nostalgic look with a future-of-chain-interaction feel, for everyone on
Hive. If someone thinks his version is stupid but improves a piece of it,
that is success.

THE TWO-LAYER INTENT (Bryan's own words)
The game is two things wearing one skin. Layer one is a front end: a fun way
to move through the chain, where real landmarks have real links that open
real chain pages, and the human makes their own choices (vote, comment,
follow) in their own hands, never automatic. Layer two is pure game:
enemies, villain, ruins, tokens, living entirely inside the game, touching
the chain nowhere. Choose-your-own-adventure: explore as you wish, partake
in game elements if you choose. The chain is never spammed. Where the game
touches the chain is always deliberate and read-only unless the user
themselves chooses to act.

THE LORE (thin by design, lands on top of geometry, never homework)
Never invent lore where the chain already provides it. The creation: builders
made a world with a three-second heartbeat, left the code open, departed;
every 30 minutes the world reweaves around what people made. Three virtues:
Power (red, stake, corrupts when hoarded), Wisdom (blue, curation), Courage
(green, posting, the newcomer's virtue); an all-power heart is how villains
and fakes are made and how you spot a bad account. The Fall and Migration:
the old world was bought by the almighty evil Emperor J SON, who seized the
21 thrones with sock puppets; the people carried the world away and left him
an empty shell (the real 2020 Steem takeover, mythologized). His name lives
in one translation string for merge-safety. Fact in the museum, myth in the
gameplay. The 21 guardians are the witnesses; Witty World is their hall.
Enemies: sock puppets (J SON's leftovers, same species as today's fakes) and
Blahs (small, loud, cannot be flagged away because where they come from there
is no downvote). Also scammer, extractor, spammer. The White Knight: an
unidentified helper who returns what was stolen (true story, parked). The
world's breath is the 30-minute reweave. The hero is the bug: a newcomer, no
stake, no reputation, just courage. One sentence: the world is alive because
real people are doing real things on a real chain right now; learn to see
them, meet them, become one of them.

DESIGN DIRECTION (settled from reference screenshots)
Illustrated theme-park map, not a diagram. Uneven globular terrain, ragged
coastline, breakout clusters, no perfect circle. The landmass silhouette is
the Hive mark eroded into terrain, glowing glassy red, dawning at map zoom,
never a billboard. Subway routes: colored, named, weighted, meaning-bearing,
kept visually distinct from soft ambient region color. Junctions are little
places with honest behavioral clues derived only from already-fetched data,
never decorative lies. Live posts are the author's real avatar ringed with
signals. Real logos wherever an account exists, via the app's own avatar
pipeline. Fictional places stay code-drawn chunky sticker: thick outlines,
flat bright fills. The bug wears a code-drawn glassy Hive mark and never
mirrors.

HARD RULES (human intent, keep enforcing)
No new repos, forks, folders, or branches unless asked. Do not touch
.claude/launch.json. Do not hardcode operation type ids. Do not call a signer
directly. Do not build verify UI or trust logic, or any feature that flags
real accounts, until the trust-model conversation happens. Do not build
around Hive Communities tooling; communities exist on the map as places but
nothing should depend on their tooling. Movement stays frozen when a pass
says so. The game broadcasts nothing.

STRATEGY AND STANCE
Meant to be shared infrastructure: any frontend could adopt the world, bug,
and rules (Snapie is the named example). Alignment not competition with
other onboarding efforts. The bar for deploying to hive.blog is GuiltyParties'
standard: not AI slop, survives real dev review. Acidyo funded a Claude
account. The upstream canonical repo is the private GitLab at
gitlab.syncad.com/hive/denser; nothing has been proposed upstream; an issue
should precede any merge request.

THE BIGGEST OPEN TRUTH
Basecamp's write path has never broadcast to mainnet. RC cost for brand-new
accounts is untested and is the most likely thing to force a redesign. The
game is immune because it broadcasts nothing. Prove the write path (rehearse
against Hoverfly's mock network, measure whether a new account can afford it,
then one real broadcast confirmed on a block explorer) before building
anything that assumes on-chain Basecamp state, including OpenAttribute.
